Component sheet

Every component the eight screens are made of, with its class name, its states, and what content it is handed. This last part is the one that matters at implementation: every list on this site is expected to grow — case studies, deep dives, employers, testimonials, technologies — so a component that cannot be given its content is a component somebody has to edit to add an item.

Assembled last on purpose. A sheet is an inventory, and an inventory taken before the set closes has to be taken again — the language switcher, the link row and the severed-seam motif all arrived after the first six screens were approved.

Specimens that depend on the palette are shown in both themes at once rather than asking you to toggle and remember. Interaction states are frozen with an is- class, because a printed sheet cannot hover.

Type ramp

Three families with three jobs. Space Grotesk is display only — the thesis, page headlines, pull quotes, the scale figure. IBM Plex Sans is everything read as prose. IBM Plex Mono is metadata: years, roles, labels, captions, anything the reader scans rather than reads. Mixing those jobs is what made the first pass read as a typeset document instead of a site.

note  display sizes carry negative tracking (−0.012 to −0.018em) because Space Grotesk sets loose at size; mono labels carry positive tracking (+0.06 to +0.11em) because uppercase mono sets tight.

Language switcher — .lang

Two locales, so a two-state switch and not a menu: a dropdown holding two options always spends a click showing one row you did not want. The alternate target is this page in the other language, never the other language’s home — being thrown back to the home page is the classic locale-switch defect, and it is a defect of data, so the target is per-page rather than a constant.

takes  current: en | es | none and alternate — the resolved URL of this page in the other locale, joined on slug per ADR-003.

07 · Controls

Contact form — four states

The one place on the site where something can fail, so it is the one component whose states have to be designed rather than inherited. Two rules do the work. On success the form is gone — an emptied form beside a success message is an invitation to send the same thing twice, and the second copy always looks worse. On failure the contents survive and another channel is named — a failed send that also loses what someone wrote costs them the message twice, and by then they are not writing it again.

The confirmation is two lines, and the second one is the address. Once it has sent, the only thing that can still be wrong is where the reply goes — so the state echoes back what was typed, and says nothing else. An earlier version explained the inbox, the absence of a ticket number and the timezone; none of that is the sender’s problem, and a confirmation that talks about itself is a confirmation nobody finishes reading.

takes  state: idle | sending | sent | error, values, fallback (the email address).
open  the backend is undecided — a Cloudflare Worker per ADR-004, or a plain mailto:. These four states are the same either way, which is why they can be designed now.

13 · Foundation

Motif vocabulary

The site’s whole visual argument, in four primitives. Strata are what cannot move — dense, horizontal, accumulated, no air between the layers. Nodes are what is new — discrete, separated, connected on purpose. The channel is the seam where they meet, and the severed channel is the 404. Nothing here is a logo or a brand, which is what lets the same geometry carry the hero, every tile motif, and eventually the diagrams.

rule  stroked geometry, never glow or gradient. A stroke survives both themes without being redesigned; a glow looks right in dark and falls apart in light. That single constraint is why the hero, the motifs and TASK 6’s eleven diagram replacements can share one language instead of three.
takes  each tile motif is referenced by id and swappable per instance — the tile does not own its drawing.

14 · Content

Logo slot — .logo-slot, .mark

Company and technology marks. Three placements, one rule. The logo is an enhancement layered on a wordmark that already reads — never a replacement for it. So an employer whose logo nobody can find is not a hole, a technology without a usable mark keeps its dot, and neither case needs a design decision when it happens.

Two consequences worth stating. The slot is a fixed box, not a natural size: a row of logos at their own dimensions has no optical baseline, and the box is what gives it one. And a placeholder is legible in a static list and reads as breakage in a moving strip — which is why the employer cards show visible slots while the marquee keeps its dots on the live screen.

takes  logo (optional), alt. Absent is a supported value, not an error state.
placements  32px in the home employer card · 38px in an Experience entry, which has the column width · 18px as a technology mark in the home stack strip.
blocked  no logo file exists yet. Sourcing them is unowned work — and it carries a licensing question per mark that a design cannot answer.
C-06  named security vendors never get a mark here. No identity provider, no liveness or fraud tooling, no OTP provider — not as a logo and not as a label. The employers are already public in the frozen content; those vendors never are.
