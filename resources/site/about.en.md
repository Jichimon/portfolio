The same problem kept finding me, across four employers that had nothing else in common: a system too important to touch, and a business that needed it to do something it was never built for.

It started at Avícola Sofía, an agro-industrial holding running production, cash and warehouse operations on Oracle EBS — instrumented for paperwork, not for the biometric attendance terminals the business needed next. I built that: [a multitenant attendance system](/case-studies/multi-tenant-biometric-attendance) across multiple plants, nationwide.

The internal systems team I was on spun out into its own company, Mamaya Tech. I went with it, and kept doing the same kind of work from the other side of that split — integrating satellite ERP systems into the same EBS core, still connected to where it all started.

Then Banco Solidario S.A. needed its mobile banking brought in-house — off a third-party vendor product, onto something the bank actually owned. My title never changed from backend developer; what changed was the scope. Within two years I was making the calls a solution architect makes: [designing the identity and payments architecture](/case-studies/mobile-banking-platform) for an app used by hundreds of thousands of people, [designing and starting the decoupling of an OTP service](/case-studies/otp-provider-decoupling) that had become everyone's single point of failure, and [migrating years of legacy payment history](/case-studies/legacy-payment-data-migration) onto the new platform without losing a record.

Most recently, Senior Software Engineer at NICE, on a globally distributed enterprise CX platform serving millions of users — legacy PHP and vanilla JavaScript running next to a modern .NET stack, the same "make the old and the new talk to each other" problem at a different scale. Working for customers across US and EU jurisdictions meant designing around how their data-protection regimes actually differ, not just checking a compliance box. I shipped features like a per-site prompt manager — gated by roles and permissions — inside the editor tenants use to build their own content, pushed a performance improvement through as an ADR instead of just as code, and spent a chunk of my time mentoring the junior half of the backend team, in English, across a genuinely global group of colleagues.

Five years is not a long career by some measures, and I don't pretend otherwise. What moved faster than the calendar was scope: from proof-of-concept work as a trainee to owning architecture decisions that hundreds of thousands of people now depend on, made under real constraints — a live system that can't go down while you rebuild it, a security review that has to sign off before anything ships, a bank that needs the business case before it needs the technical one. Judgment isn't a title. It's having made enough of those calls, in public, with real consequences, to know which questions to ask before the ones that come back to bite you.

I'm still finishing a degree in Ingeniería Informática at Universidad Autónoma Gabriel René Moreno (UAGRM), started in 2017 — paused for now, but I plan on finishing it. Teaching is where I'd like to take it, eventually.

Outside of work, the same instinct shows up in sport: climbing and hiking around Bolivia (Huayna Potosí included), and amateur boxing — I just like being bad at something new until I'm not.
